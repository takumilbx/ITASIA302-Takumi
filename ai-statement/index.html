How these pages were made

Takumi  ·  UTokyo  ·  Spring 2026

Every page on this site was produced with help from AI tools. This is the page where I say which ones, what they did, and what I still insist on doing myself.

03.1  ·  The principle

My thought processwhile using AI

A lot of AI disclosure right now is a paragraph at the bottom of a page that says AI was used in the creation of this work and then leaves you with no way to tell what that means. Not only does it not help you evaluate the work, it also does not help you understand how the work was made (and for me to copy their working style and produce mine too lol).

So, specifically I use AI for the tasks I am not capable of doing despite 23 years (24 this year, thanks for asking) of being alive on this planet. That list includes, in no particular order of embarrassment: programming languages (I started learning Python this year and I think Python is starting to tolerate me), HTML, CSS, web design, and what I will collectively file under other coding stuff really, I am trying to learn. The site you are reading was built by someone who is still quietly googling what flex-direction does.

What follows is the long-form version of that confession: every tool in the kit, what I pointed it at, and a per-assignment split of who did what. I would rather be specific and slightly embarrassing than vague and impressively, technically correct.

03.3  ·  Per-assignment breakdown

What I did  /  what the models did

For each piece on this site, here is the split. Nothing hidden, nothing dressed up.

Research Trends Class Assignment  ·  Longform scroll

What I did

  • The five-year window came with the course, not from me so that one is not a personal flex.
  • Wrote the actual content analysis code that categorizes each paper into trend buckets. The categories are mine, the scaffolding is mine, the bugs were also mine.
  • Used Claude to help with scraping the source list, then rechecked every number by hand against the original bibliography. I did not trust counts I had not recounted.
  • Read the bibliography and picked which threads were worth tracing.
  • Wrote every claim about the meaning of the shifts.
  • Designed the scroll pacing and the tone.

What the tools did

  • Claude  helped me write the scraping script and later the content-analysis code (I still had to tell it what I wanted).
  • NotebookLM  (called via Claude's MCP server) surfaced terminology trends across the source set without inventing citations.
  • Claude  drafted HTML/CSS and iterated on copy when asked.
  • Grammarly  do grammar and not make me look like an IELTS BAND 5.5 student.
  • Claude Design / Stitch  offered layout variants I pulled from.
Reading Response Class Assignment  ·  Week 3

What I did

  • Picked the reading that actually overlapped with my own research interests.
  • Read the whole thing myself, in a chair, with freshly dripped coffee (you're welcome to try).
  • Then used Claude (through its NotebookLM MCP and learning mode) to re-walk the paper and argue with me about it.
  • Pulled the discussion back toward my research interests and wrote the position from there.

What the tools did

  • Claude  in learning mode walked me back through the paper question by question.
  • NotebookLM  (via the MCP connection) let Claude answer only from the paper itself, not from general memory.
  • Grammarly  again, make me look like a native speaker.
  • Claude Design  suggested the theme-tile visual structure.
Personal Project Independent work  ·  In progress

What I did

  • Picked the topic and what the project is actually trying to do.
  • Am still working out the shape of the argument.
  • Deliberately held the page back rather than posting a clean placeholder that would misrepresent where I am.

What the tools did

  • Claude  is helping me stress-test framings in conversation.
  • No AI-generated abstract is on the page, by choice, until I have written one I stand behind.
The site itself Infrastructure  ·  Design system

What I did

  • Decided the information architecture and URL structure.
  • Reviewed every page at multiple viewport sizes and flagged regressions.

03.4  ·  What I still do

The parts I will not let AI do

A short list of things that stay with me even when a model helped produce the sentences around them.

The position. Whether the reading response takes side A or side B is my call. I do not ask a model what it thinks and then quietly inherit the answer; I decide what I think, and then use models to test whether my reasoning holds up.

The sources. I do not let models invent citations. If a claim is sourced, I have read the source. NotebookLM is useful precisely because it can only speak from the documents I give it and cannot invent anything beyond them.

The accountability. If a sentence on this site is wrong, self-important, or just badly argued, that is on me. AI tools are happy to sit next to me while the work is going well and mysteriously vanish the moment someone spots a typo. That is not a split I am willing to accept. Credit goes where it goes, but every mistake on this page has exactly one author (me).

03.5  ·  Closing

A note to anyone that is still here

If you are reading this, the honest summary is: I used AI extensively, and I would have produced worse work without it.

I want to tie that line to something more specific than productivity. I am, first, a learner. From that seat, these tools have mostly done two useful things for me: they let my ideas take an actual shape on the page, and they quietly covered for the places I still do not know enough.

And nevertheless (I like that word), I am stubbornly still learning the fundamentals in parallel Python, CSS, HTML, the parts of web design that are less glamorous than they sound. The plan is not to ride on top of these tools forever while pretending to understand what is underneath (otherwise there will be no seat for me in the job market T^T). The plan is to build enough of the underlying skill that, a few years from now when these tools are even faster and weirder, I can steer them instead of the other way around.
