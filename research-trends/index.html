01  ·  Volume

Output nearly doubled in a single year.

From 2021 to 2024 the journal averaged roughly 250 articles a year. Then, in 2025, it published 643 more than any previous twelve months and almost as many as the three years before it, combined.

+94% YoY The 2025 surge is not incremental growth it is a phase change. The field didn't grow; it accelerated.
02  ·  The GenAI Surge

From nowhere to one in four in three years.

In 2021, essentially no articles engaged with what we now call generative AI the vocabulary did not yet exist. After ChatGPT's launch in November 2022, the share climbs almost linearly. By 2025, one in four papers in the journal reads AI through the GenAI lens.

03  ·  Five trajectories

Five topics. Five different stories.

Zoom out from generative AI and the field's attention map is more varied than it looks. Ethics holds steady as the constant anchor. Creativity turns out to be the quiet second pillar. Bias climbs slowly. Robots collapse. Education barely moves. Click any topic below to isolate its trajectory.

05  ·  So what

A field is what it chooses to study.

I scraped 1,849 articles for this page and sat with the numbers for a while. What surprised me wasn't any single statistic. It was how often the journal seems to swap what it cares about. Roughly every eighteen months, the urgent question changes. These are the four topic changes in this journal that I found it interesting.

2021–2022

Robots, ethics, and a pandemic.

When I started reading the 2021 archive, I was expecting a lot of algorithm talk. What I actually found was robots. Robots & HRI is the single biggest theme of 2021 at 18.9%, about one in five papers that year. Next to it, Ethics & Governance at 25.5%, mostly about moral-responsibility and agency.

What I didn't expect was a whole cluster on archives, email collections, born-digital records, digital preservation, and a little bit on COVID-era digital life.

2023

Something shifts.

Then 2023 reads like a different journal. Ethics is still the constant at 26.6%, but the conversation underneath it changes. I kept running into the word coloniality. Representation bias. Who actually controls these systems. The field stops asking is this ethical? and starts asking whose ethics, shaped by whom?

2024–2025

And then ChatGPT.

Two years after ChatGPT launched, the literature finally shows up. Generative AI goes from a vocabulary that essentially did not exist in 2021 to one in four papers in 2025 (24.0%). Volume nearly doubles too, with 203 articles in 2023 to 643 in 2025.

What I noticed about this writing is that it isn't speculative anymore. It is no longer what if LLMs could do X? It is the tools are already here, here is what they are doing to students' essays, to artists' studios, to journalists' drafts.

2026 partial year

Something shifts again.

The first 196 articles of 2026 (January through mid-April) look different again. Ethics & Governance jumps to about 38%, a ten-point leap from 2025. Governance as a keyword shows up in 22% of submissions, accountability in 12%. Generative AI dips to around 20%.

From my understanding (even though it's been only 4 months), the field has stopped asking what can these systems do? and started asking how do we do damage control?

Two things don't really change these 5 year. A quarter of every year's pages always goes to ethics and governance. And creativity and the arts, music, literature, aesthetics sit at about 17% every year.

But there's a third pattern I want to raise, because it's the one I'm actually writing a thesis in, which is the classroom. Even across all 1,653 articles, education stays a minority theme.

06  ·  The classroom

The place we meet these systems first.

Of all the patterns in the archive, education is the one I keep staring at. It's never a major theme, and its never more than about 14% of any year. But the shift of its trend is strange, and I think it says something about how this field reacts to what's happening in the real world.

2021 6.6% ~13 of 196 articles
2022 6.4% ~18 of 280 articles
2023 2.5% ~5 of 203 articles
2024 7.9% ~26 of 331 articles
2025 9.8% 63 of 643 articles
2026 · partial ~14% 28 of 196 articles to date

The 2023 dip.

In 2023, only five of 203 articles touched education by the strict definition I used. That's 2.5%, the lowest share for any topic in any year, except Robots in 2025. And it's the same year the journal pivoted to coloniality and power.

I don't think the classroom suddenly mattered less. I think the field's attention just went somewhere else, and honestly maybe rightly so. But it does mean that when ChatGPT landed a few months later, the education literature was starting from almost nothing.

The post-ChatGPT climb.

Then education comes back and keeps climbing. 2.5% in 2023, 7.9% in 2024, 9.8% in 2025, and about 14% in the first four months of 2026. In raw numbers that's a ~12× jump from 2023 to 2025, and 2026 is already outpacing the rest.

ChatGPT affected how students do their homework in late 2022. The literature took roughly two years to catch up. Two years is a long time when a tool is already in every classroom.

Why I keep writing about this.

Even at this peak, education is still less than one in seven articles.

But the place most students and teachers are actually meeting these tools first is the classroom, and we might ask who designs the lesson when the tool writes the answer? This question is still being worked out in real time, by actual teachers, with almost no guidance from the literature. My reading response on Polat & Sarıdaş, 31 March 2026, is one of the 28 2026 articles in this count. So keep on reading if you're interested in how AI is shaping education.

Topic definition Strict matches on teacher, student, classroom, pedagogy, curriculum, higher education, AI literacy, and related terms. Bare learning was excluded because it would match machine learning on nearly every article. Five-year span Roughly 125 education-tagged articles total (2021–2025). If that share holds, 2026 will be the first year education out-ranks policy, bias, and (possibly) creativity. 07  ·  Methodology appendix

How these numbers were built.

A companion to the visualization: what was counted, how topics were detected, and where the method stops short. The full text version lives in methodology.md alongside this page.

Corpus

  • Journal: AI & Society, Springer Nature.
  • Scraping window: all articles with publication date ≥ 2021-01-01 present in the online archive at the time of collection.
  • Analysis window: calendar years 2021 through 2025 inclusive (five full years). Articles dated 2026-01 to 2026-04 are present in the underlying dataset but excluded because 2026 is incomplete at the time of analysis.
  • N analysed: 1,653 articles.
  • Fields used: Title (never null); Abstract (null in 549 of 1,849 full-dataset rows treated as empty strings for matching); Date Published.

Topic classification

Each article is checked against a set of regex patterns for each of eight topics. A match in either the title or the abstract tags the article with that topic. Articles may carry multiple topics (overlap is permitted); percentages are the share of that year’s articles where the topic was detected and do not sum to 100.

Design principles

  1. Word boundaries (\b) on every pattern to avoid substring false positives bias does not match biasing or unbiased unless explicitly allowed.
  2. Compound phrases over bare common words where a word is too generic on its own. \bwork\b alone matches hundreds of irrelevant articles (“this work argues”, “framework”, “worked on”); the tightened work pattern requires compounds such as labour market, future of work, workplace, gig economy, workforce, employment.
  3. Cross-topic hygiene. Each word belongs to exactly one topic. regulation is a policy word, not an ethics word; governance is an ethics word, not a policy word. Without this discipline, Ethics and Policy would double-count roughly half of each other’s articles.
  4. Morphological variants through regex alternation (robot|robotic|robots|robotics), not through substring matching.

Limitations

  1. Title + abstract only. Full-text was not available through scraping. A topic discussed in the body but not mentioned in the abstract will not be detected. This biases the analysis toward topics that appear in article framing.
  2. English only. All patterns are English; non-English articles (a small minority) will under-match.
  3. Keyword precision is a design choice. A looser keyword set (bare work, bare learning, bare design) inflates topics at the cost of precision; a stricter set loses genuine-but-unusually-phrased matches. This report errs on the side of precision. A looser pass tested earlier produced dramatically different rankings (Work appeared as 42% of 2025 rather than 4.4%, driven almost entirely by incidental “work” usage).
  4. Overlap is allowed by design. A paper on “fair GenAI in education” tags three topics. Percentages therefore quantify engagement, not exclusive topic share.
  5. 2025 volume is genuine but skewed. 643 articles is an unusually large year for the journal. Patterns observed for 2025 may partly reflect editorial or indexing changes at the publisher as well as scholarly attention.

Reproducibility

The analysis is implemented in step3_categorize.py (permissive baseline) and step5_tight.py (final strict set, used for this report). Both operate directly on the source xlsx AI_SOCIETY_Articles_2021_onwards.xlsx. The regex patterns listed above are the exact patterns used in step5_tight.py.
