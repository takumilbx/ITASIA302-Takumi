Coverage span
Articles were collected from the full publication archives of both journals. Titles, abstracts, and publication years were extracted. Only research articles were included; editorials, conference reports, and book reviews were excluded.
Theme coding
A taxonomy of 42 themes was developed bottom-up from the vocabulary present in article titles and abstracts, then grouped into seven conceptual clusters.
TECHNOLOGY
ICT · e-Learning · Tablets · Mobile · SNS · VR · Games · AI · GenAI · Analytics
ASSESSMENT

PEDAGOGY
CSCL · Community · Active learning · PBL · Motivation · Self-regulation
MEDIA
AV broadcast · Video · Multimedia · Instructional design
TEACHER
Pre-service · In-service (FD/SD) · Reflection · Observation · Expertise
CONTEXT
Special needs · Curriculum · Policy · Literacy · STEM · School levels · HE · Adult
Each article received one or more theme labels. Labelling was performed by keyword matching against a controlled vocabulary list, then manually reviewed for ambiguous cases. An article tagged with n themes contributes 1 count to each of those n theme totals.
Trend lines (tab 01 & 02)
Each year's value for a given theme is the share of that year's articles that carry the theme, not a raw count. This normalises for uneven publication volume across decades.
sharetheme,year = articles tagged (theme, year) all articles in year × 100
expressed as %
The raw per-year percentage is stored in RAW.smoothed. Years with zero articles for that theme are recorded as 0. Early years (1977–1983) often have very small annual totals, so a single article can register as 8–25%, so treat those values with caution.
Inflection detection (tab 02)
Rise = year-over-year increase ≥ threshold. Collapse = symmetric decline. Peak = maximum value across all years. Thresholds were tuned per theme to suppress noise in sparse early years.
Co-occurrence (tabs 03 & 04)
For every pair of themes (A, B), the Jaccard similarity measures how often they appear together relative to how often either appears at all, a size-normalised co-occurrence score.
J(A,B) = |A ∩ B| |A ∪ B| = |A ∩ B| |A| + |B| − |A ∩ B|
range 0 (no overlap) → 1 (identical sets)
|A| and |B| are the total article counts for each theme (from RAW.theme_totals); |A ∩ B| is the count of articles tagged with both. All 42 × 42 pairs are stored in RAW.jac_matrix.
Heatmap cell colour
Linear interpolation from J = 0 to J = 0.13. Cells above 0.13 are clamped to max amber.
Overlap count in tooltip
Derived on hover as J·(|A|+|B|)/(1+J) and rounded, an approximation since exact counts are not stored separately.
Limitations & caveats
Sparse early years
Publication counts before ~1990 are small (sometimes < 10/year). A single article can spike a theme's annual share to 10–25%. Treat pre-1990 trend values as indicative only.
Two journals only
Coverage is limited to JMET and JJET. Other Japanese edtech venues (conference proceedings, book chapters, regional journals) are not included. International literature is out of scope.
Theme boundary ambiguity
The 42-theme taxonomy involves judgment calls. Some themes overlap conceptually (e.g. e-Learning vs. Online Learning). Articles on the boundary may be inconsistently coded, inflating or suppressing certain Jaccard values.
No citation weighting
Every article is counted equally regardless of impact, citations, or methodological rigour. A heavily cited landmark paper counts the same as a short report.
Jaccard is symmetric
J(A, B) = J(B, A) by definition. The heatmap is therefore mirrored across the diagonal. This hides directionality: it can't tell you whether theme A leads theme B or vice versa.

This study
"How do K–12 teachers enact instructional design when implementing 1:1 device programs, and what learning environments do they construct as a result?"

This question occupies the intersection of two understudied dimensions: the instructional design process as teacher practice (not merely teacher knowledge), and the learning environment as a holistically designed system. No prior JMET/JJET paper examines both together in a GIGA-context classroom.

National policy context
How policy shaped the research landscape
31 headline events · 2000–2026 · 4 thematic threads

Japan's educational technology landscape cannot be read from publication counts alone. Behind the citation curves lies a sequence of deliberate national interventions: infrastructure mandates, curriculum revisions, and capacity-building programs that redirected what researchers studied and when. This panel overlays 31 headline policy events (2000–2026) onto the same JMET + JJET trend data, making visible the structural relationship between government policy and academic inquiry.

Four phases emerge: the e-Japan era (2000–2007) established basic connectivity targets; a 21st-century skills wave (2008–2015) embedded ICT as cross-curricular competency without closing the hardware gap; the Society 5.0 → GIGA pivot (2016–2019) set a 1:1 device mandate; and post-GIGA deployment (2020–present) shifted urgency from infrastructure to classroom practice, which is exactly where this thesis situates itself.

Four policy phases
From connectivity to pedagogy
Phase 1 · 2000–2007
e-Japan & Early Pilots

The IT Basic Act (2000) and e-Japan Strategy (2001) launched Japan's first systematic school connectivity campaign. The 2003 mandating of high-school Informatics gave researchers their first policy-driven curriculum variable. The PISA 2003 score drop triggered anxiety about outcomes, shifting research attention toward measurable learning effects for the first time.

Phase 2 · 2008–2015
21st-Century Skills Wave

The 2008 Basic Plan for Education and Course of Study revision embedded ICT as a cross-curricular competency, and collaborative learning, problem-solving, and information literacy appeared in the research literature in earnest. Yet MEXT surveys (2013) revealed only 35% of classrooms had computers, exposing a persistent policy–infrastructure gap that research spent the decade trying to characterise.

Phase 3 · 2016–2019
Society 5.0 → GIGA

The Society 5.0 vision (2016) reframed education as critical infrastructure for a data-driven economy. The 2017 Course of Study mandated programming in elementary schools; PISA 2018 simultaneously flagged Japan as lowest in OECD for in-class ICT use. The GIGA School launch in 2019, which provided 1 device per student across 9.2M pupils, catalysed a sharp spike in device-related and 1:1-learning research that is still running.

Phase 4 · 2020–present
GIGA Deployment & Pedagogical Turn

COVID-19 accelerated GIGA by three years. Device deployment reached near-universality within months, but speed revealed the pedagogical gap: research began documenting not just that devices were present, but how teachers were, or were not, integrating them into lesson design. The Phase 2 infrastructure fund (¥266B, 2023) and the AI Promotion Act (2025) signal that this question will define the field through the late 2020s. This thesis enters that gap directly.
