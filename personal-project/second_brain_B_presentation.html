01 · Personal Knowledge Management / definition

What is Personal Knowledge Management?

  • A real system for your own knowledge: collect, store, retrieve.
  • The point is custody: you run it, not a company or institution.
  • Success isn't how much you save. It's finding the one note you need, the moment you need it.
Aside · the habit is old
  • Term is new (Frand & Hixon, 1999 UCLA).
  • Habit is centuries old: Leonardo's notebooks, Luhmann's 90,000-card Zettelkasten.
  • Bush's 1945 Memex pulled reading back along named trails (Bush, 1945). The dream was never storage. It was retrieval.
02 · Second Brain

And a second brain?

  • The famous version of the idea: a digital place for everything you collect.
  • But storage isn't the point.
  • A folder of saved articles is just a digital attic.
  • It's supposed to be a thinking partner (Forte).
Tipped-in · 1998
"Thinking does not only happen inside the head." In "The Extended Mind", Andy Clark and David Chalmers argue that if Otto trusts his notebook and reaches for it the way you reach for memory, the notebook becomes part of his mind. Moving what you know into a system you trust is not laziness, it is cognitive extension. (Clark and Chalmers, 1998)
03 · The Hype

Everyone is selling a brain

  • Everyone sells a brain: bestseller books, a creator economy demoing setups.
  • Newest promise (Aal & Rüller, 2025): AI makes it effortless, a companion that thinks for you.
  • Whether it makes you think better? Almost nobody has the proof.
Failure · 01
The Productivity Trap

the system needs so much upkeep you spend more time maintaining it than thinking with it.

04 · The Meme A beautifully tagged graveyard
The people who sell you a "second brain" often cannot remember where their own car keys are. The partner who quietly tracks the keys, the glasses, and the appointments was the original second brain all along. based on the "second brain" meme by @nunoei, paraphrased
Why this project exists
  • I came to this through failure, not theory.
  • Organizing was always a job for Future Me, who never showed up. A beautifully tagged graveyard.
  • The industry's answer is always the next template, the next app.
  • So: is the failure mine, or the method's?
05 · What I am building

A second brain that actually helps me think

  • Not a fancier filing cabinet.
  • Small single-idea notes that connect into arguments.
  • Most advice obsesses over capture. What decides success is retrieval.
  • Recall teaches more than re-reading (Roediger & Karpicke, 2006).
  • So: retrieval first, then test the claim honestly.

07 · Zettelkasten

The slip-box

  • Luhmann fed a wooden slip-box for 30+ years, past 90,000 cards.
  • One idea per card, his own words, fixed address (e.g. 21/3d).
  • Filed by link, not topic. Structure emerges, never imposed.
  • He called it a conversation partner: ask it, it answers with forgotten connections.
  • Atom, molecule, alloy = the same idea, rebuilt in plain text.

Luhmann, "Communicating with Slip Boxes", 1981

08 · The Method

The framework I apply

A Zettelkasten built retrieval-first, not capture-first. Knowledge climbs three tiers; nothing moves up until the tier below holds.
