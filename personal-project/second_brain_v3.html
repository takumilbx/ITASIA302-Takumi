Building a Second Brain
(For Real This Time)

The project in one walk-through. What PKM is, why the hype around it is suspicious, what I am building, and where it is heading.

01 · PKM

What is PKM?

Personal knowledge management is the set of steps a person uses to collect, organize, store, find, and share what they know. It means having a real system for your own knowledge instead of just hoping you will remember things later. The key idea is that the individual, not a company, is in charge of managing it.

The habit is old

The term comes from a 1999 paper by Frand and Hixon, but the practice runs from Leonardo's notebooks to Luhmann's Zettelkasten, a box of more than 90,000 linked cards. In 1945, Vannevar Bush imagined the Memex, a desk that could store everything you read and find it again quickly.

02 · Second Brain

And a second brain?

If PKM is the general idea, the second brain is its most famous version: an external, digital place for the ideas you collect. But storage is not the point. Tiago Forte calls it a system that "expands our memory and our intellect using the modern tools of technology and networks." A folder of saved articles is a digital attic. A second brain is meant to be a thinking partner.

The extended mind

Clark and Chalmers argued in 1998 that thinking does not only happen inside the head. If Otto trusts his notebook and uses it constantly, it becomes part of his mind. Moving information into a system you trust is not laziness, it is cognitive extension.

03 · The Hype

Everyone is selling a brain

Spend any time in the productivity side of the internet and someone will try to sell you a brain. Forte's book was a bestseller. Creators show off note-taking systems with the energy people usually save for skincare routines. The newest promise is that AI will finally make it effortless. Whether any of it actually works is treated as a separate, slightly awkward question.

04 · The Meme

The people who promote "second brains" often still cannot remember where their own car keys are. The partner who quietly tracks the keys, the glasses, and the appointments was the original second brain all along.

based on the "second brain" meme by @nunoei, paraphrased

I came to this not through theory but through failure. For years, organizing my notes was a job for Future Me, who never showed up. My vault turned into a beautifully tagged graveyard. Is the failure mine, or the method's? That is the whole reason for this project.

05 · What I'm Building

A second brain that actually helps me think

I am not building a fancier filing cabinet. I am building a system where small, single-idea notes connect into arguments, and where the question "do I really understand this?" has a structural answer. Most PKM advice focuses on capture, getting everything in. But the thing that decides whether a system works is retrieval, getting the right idea back out at the moment you are thinking. So the project puts retrieval first, then tests that idea honestly.

06 · Current State

Where I am now

A paper enters through Zotero, becomes a literature note in Obsidian, and Claude Code runs a NotebookLM integration to extract candidate atoms. Verified atoms land in the vault, cluster into molecules, and synthesize into alloys tracked in Notion. This is that vault, in full.

Step 01 · Zotero
Capture the paper
Every source enters through Zotero. PDF attached, metadata extracted, citekey auto-generated authorYEAR. A structured import note drops into the vault inbox.
Step 02 · Obsidian
Process into a literature note
The import becomes a structured note @FERREIRA2026.md. Four sections, always: Main Argument, Methodology, Key Findings, Atoms Extracted. The single source of truth downstream.
Step 03 · Claude + NotebookLM
Extract atoms via NotebookLM
Claude Code reads the note, opens the PDF, runs the NotebookLM integration. Focused queries return candidate atoms. Each is verified against the source; verified atoms saved, rejected ones logged with reasons.
Step 04 · Atoms
Verified atoms land in the vault
Each becomes its own file. The filename is the claim as a full declarative sentence, findable by search alone. The vault holds atoms across thesis topics: TPACK, PKM, Japan's GIGA program, Bangkok's BMA pilot.
Step 05 · Molecules
Atoms cluster into molecules
When 2 to 5 atoms share a theme, they pull into a molecule: a connection note with no new claims, only relations. Pattern recognition stops being implicit and becomes a saved object.
Step 06 · Alloys
Molecules fuse into alloys
An alloy is a thesis-grade synthesis, an original argument built from molecules and atoms. It can never be drafted directly from a source. Alloys are where the research output lives.
Step 07 · Notion
Alloys surface in Notion
Each alloy becomes a thesis chapter draft or a fieldwork milestone. Deadlines and status reference vault notes by citekey. Obsidian is the brain. Notion is the calendar.
