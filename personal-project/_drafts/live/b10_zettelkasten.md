## [blist3] blist
- By the time Luhmann wrote about his slip-box, he had worked with it for 26 years [[(Luhmann, 1981, p. 223)]]. Over its full life, 1951 to 1997, it grew to roughly 90,000 cards [[(Schmidt, 2016)]].
- Every slip carries a fixed number that never changes, and the filing deliberately runs against topical order [[(Luhmann, 1981, pp. 223-224)]].
- Each note "gains its quality from the network of references and back-references." Structure *emerges*. It is never imposed [[(Luhmann, 1981, p. 225)]].
- His precondition for communication: "the partners can surprise each other mutually." The box is a partner, a second memory [[(Luhmann, 1981, pp. 222, 225)]].
- Atom, molecule, alloy: my plain-text rebuild of that idea, with one rule Luhmann never wrote down: one idea per note.

## [essay4] essay · Field note
The ninety thousand cards are not what convinced me. Luhmann wrote about the box as something that could surprise him, and I had never thought about notes that way. Mine started doing it somewhere past the thousandth atom. I searched for something about teacher judgment and got back a note I had no memory of writing, sitting next to two I did. I still cannot tell you how to make that happen sooner.
