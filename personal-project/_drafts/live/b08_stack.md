## [body3] body
Five tools, in the order work moves through them. A source enters left, exits as a note you can find.

## [blist4] blist
- *Zotero* · capture. Every source enters with its PDF, metadata, and citekey; a structured import note drops into the vault inbox.
- *Obsidian* · the vault. Plain-text notes linked into a graph, reached by search and backlink, not folders.
- *Claude Code* · the operator. Reads the literature note, opens the PDF, runs extraction, verifies every candidate against the source.
- *NotebookLM* · the extractor. Focused queries that can only answer from the given source, so claims stay honest.
- *Notion* · the calendar. Chapters, milestones, deadlines, linked back by citekey. Obsidian is the brain. Notion is the calendar.

## [essay5] essay · Field note
The stack looks like tool clutter until you notice each tool holds a different kind of trust. Zotero I trust with provenance. Obsidian I trust with meaning. The AI layer I deliberately do not trust: everything it extracts gets checked against the source page before it becomes an atom. That distrust is a feature. An assistant that can only answer from the paper in front of it cannot invent my thesis for me.
