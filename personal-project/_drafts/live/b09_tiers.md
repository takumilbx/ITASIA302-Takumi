## [body3] body
The system breaks what you read into small pieces and then links them. I use the names for those pieces from a video explaining the method [[(Wanderloots, 2024)]]. The smallest is an atom. One atom is one claim, or one fact, from one source. When I read literature, or when I have an idea, I note it as an atom.

## [body4] body
From there, I link the atoms that share a concept into a cluster of ideas called molecules. No new argument gets made at this stage. Luhmann's point was that a note "gains its quality from the network of references and back-references" [[(Luhmann, 1981, p. 225)]], so the linking is the work.

## [body5] body
Then I combine molecules into a bigger chunk called alloys, which is where a new argument comes from, something I have thought of myself. Luhmann set one condition for the box, that "the partners can surprise each other mutually" [[(Luhmann, 1981, pp. 222, 225)]]. That is the process I use to organise my thoughts and to keep track of my own thinking.
