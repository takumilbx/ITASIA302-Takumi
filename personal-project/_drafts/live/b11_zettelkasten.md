## [blist3] blist
- Luhmann had worked with his slip-box for 26 years before he wrote about it [[(Luhmann, 1981, p. 223)]]. Across its whole life, 1951 to 1997, it reached roughly 90,000 cards [[(Schmidt, 2016)]].
- Every slip carries a fixed number that never changes, and the filing deliberately runs against topical order [[(Luhmann, 1981, pp. 223-224)]].
- Each note "gains its quality from the network of references and back-references" [[(Luhmann, 1981, p. 225)]]. Structure *emerges* rather than being imposed.
- He set one condition for communication, that "the partners can surprise each other mutually" [[(Luhmann, 1981, pp. 222, 225)]]. He meant the box could hold up its end.
- Atom, molecule and alloy are my plain text rebuild of that, with one rule Luhmann never wrote down. One idea per note.

## [essay4] essay · Field note
The ninety thousand cards are not what convinced me. Luhmann wrote about the box as something that could surprise him, and I had never thought about notes that way. Mine started doing it somewhere past the thousandth atom. I searched for something about teacher judgment and got back a note I had no memory of writing, sitting next to two I did. I still cannot tell you how to make that happen sooner.
