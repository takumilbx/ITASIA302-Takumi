## [h22] h2
What is {{PKM}}?

## [blist3] blist
- A real system for your own knowledge: collect, store, *retrieve*. The point is custody. You run it, not a company.
- The term is young: it traces to Frand and Hixson's paper at EDUCOM '98 [[(Frand & Hixson, 1998)]]. The habit is centuries old: Leonardo left more than 7,000 notebook pages mixing anatomy, engineering, and art [[(Isaacson, 2017)]]; Luhmann's slip-box grew to some 90,000 cards [[(Schmidt, 2016)]].
- In 1945 Vannevar Bush imagined the Memex: a desk that stored everything you read and pulled it back along associative trails. The dream was never storage. It was retrieval [[(Bush, 1945, sect. 6)]].
- Once Drucker introduced the idea of knowledge work, managing what you know stopped being optional [[(Drucker, 1959)]].

## [essay4] essay · Field note
The library-versus-pile image is the one that finally landed for me. Both hold all your books. Only one gives you the right book before a deadline. For most of my degree I owned a pile and called it a library. The difference is not effort or discipline. It is whether the system is built for putting things in or for getting things back out.
