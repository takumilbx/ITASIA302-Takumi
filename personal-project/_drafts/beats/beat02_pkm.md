# Beat 02 · Personal Knowledge Management

## Copy
- Meta: 02 · Personal Knowledge Management / definition
- Headline: What is *Personal Knowledge Management*?
- Bullets:
  - A real system for your own knowledge: collect, store, *retrieve*.
  - The point is *custody*: you run it, not a company or an institution.
  - Success is not how much you save. It is finding the one note you need, the moment you need it.
- Aside card (the habit is old):
  - The term is young: it traces to Frand and Hixson's late-1990s UCLA work (corrected spelling: Hixson; presented at EDUCOM '98) [cite:WEB-06].
  - The habit is centuries old. Leonardo kept notebooks [cite:WEB-10]. Luhmann kept a box of slips that grew past ninety thousand cards [cite:LUHM-01].
  - In 1945 Vannevar Bush imagined the Memex, a desk that stored everything you read and pulled it back along named trails [cite:WEB-01]. The dream was never storage. It was retrieval.
  - Once Drucker described the knowledge worker in 1959, someone whose main asset is what they know, managing that knowledge stopped being optional [cite:WEB-07, Landmarks of Tomorrow, hedge "coined"].

## Personal essay
The library-versus-pile image is the one that finally landed for me. Both hold all your books. Only one gives you the right book before a deadline. For most of my degree I owned a pile and called it a library. The difference is not effort or discipline. It is whether the system is built for putting things in or for getting things back out.
