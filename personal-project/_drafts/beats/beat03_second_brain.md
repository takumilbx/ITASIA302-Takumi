In "The Extended Mind", Clark and Chalmers argue that if Otto trusts his notebook and reaches for it the way you reach for memory, the notebook becomes part of his mind. Moving what you know into a system you trust is not laziness. It is cognitive extension [cite:WEB-02].

## Personal essay
The extended mind argument is the philosophical permission slip I needed. I used to feel vaguely guilty about looking things up in my own notes, as if real understanding meant holding everything in my skull. Clark and Chalmers dissolve that guilt. The question is not whether the knowledge sits in my head or in the vault. The question is whether I trust the vault enough to reach for it without thinking. That trust has to be earned by the system, and most systems never earn it.
